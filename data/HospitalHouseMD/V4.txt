HospitalHouseMD treats patients presenting rare and frequently unexplained symptoms. Each patient is allocated at least one doctor to work toward a diagnosis. The hospital's database lists every possible treatment for each illness. These treatments may be applied to any diagnosis and then carried out. Before treatment begins, the head doctor must approve the diagnosis reached by another doctor.

Treatment has three possible stages. Stage 1 is the weakest and can begin immediately without patient consent. If it is ineffective, the treatment must progress to stage 2 or possibly straight to stage 3, subject to the rules below. Leaving stage 1 for a higher stage requires the patient's consent, provided by signing a form that authorizes escalation of their treatments. This consent covers any treatment applied to the patient, so escalation does not require a new request each time. Once the form is signed, all the patient's treatments may move freely from stage 1 to stages 2 and 3 until consent is withdrawn.

A patient receiving a stage-3 treatment needs more frequent follow-up, with nurses and doctors checking more often. When a particular treatment for a patient no longer requires this enhanced monitoring, a doctor is responsible for moving it back down to a lower stage.

Illnesses also carry priority scores to help avert outbreaks, for example of unknown viruses. A score can be changed at any time. The hospital currently recognizes only high and low priorities. Assigning high priority to an illness immediately escalates every applied treatment for it to stage 3 through a priority stage upgrade. This high-priority escalation to stage 3 does not require patient consent.
