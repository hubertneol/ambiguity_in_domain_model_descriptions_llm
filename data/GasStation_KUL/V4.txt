An oil company owns gas stations across the country. Customers may pay for refueling with cash at the counter or with their personal credit cards, and they can also request a refuel card. Using the refuel card defers payment. At each month's end, the cardholder receives an invoice listing the amounts for all refueling paid with that card. Discounts on this invoice depend on the customer's turnover during the month being invoiced.

Invoices and payments need not follow a single sequence: a customer may receive a second invoice before paying the first. Payment for an invoice can, however, be received only after that invoice has been sent. A refueling transaction appears solely on the invoice for its own month. If the customer leaves an invoice unpaid, those transactions are not carried over to the following month's invoice. Instead, the original invoice is resent as a reminder. Repeated failure to pay—for example, in two consecutive months—results in suspension of the customer and blocking of the card until the bills are paid. The blocking threshold is not entirely fixed, since very good customers receive more leeway than new ones. Paying the outstanding bills allows a return to normal status.

A station has several pumps, each containing a particular gasoline type. A pump can serve only one customer refueling a car at a time. Removing the nozzle permits refueling to begin, and returning it stops refueling. No other customer may use that pump in the intervening period. Once the nozzle is back, the next customer can use it.

Each pump has its own reservoir. When gasoline falls below a preset reorder level, that reservoir must be refilled, and refilling may take place at any time. A daily system report must identify the pumps needing refilling and give every pump's current gasoline level and required order quantity. Dirt may accumulate at the bottom of a tank. Therefore, falling below a critical fuel level automatically blocks the pump and prevents refueling. For the same reason, refilling a tank whose level was still above that critical point causes the person responsible for the gas station to block its pump for roughly an hour while dirt settles. That person releases the pump once refueling is considered safe again.
