A film set employs several people, each with a known name and social security number. Creative employees include actors and directors. The education an actor has completed is known, and the name of a director's assistant is recorded. Both actors and directors may have won an Oscar, so their Oscar status is stored. Technicians also work on the set, and each technician's field of activity is known.

Every set occupies a particular location. Several films can use the same set, and an individual film may be shot across several sets. A film records its title and release year and is assigned one particular genre from thriller, action, horror, and comedy.

For screenwriters, records contain a name and the most successful film. A screenwriter produces several screenplays, but a screenplay originates with exactly one screenwriter. Its known details include title, version, date, scene count, and plot. The basis of a screenplay is either a novel or a concept; a novel has a title and an author. A film made on a set uses a particular screenplay, and that screenplay is written for precisely one film. Actors appearing in the film must read the screenplay to prepare for their roles. The film's director subsequently realizes the screenplay in accordance with the specifications.
