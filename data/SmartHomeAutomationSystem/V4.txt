A smart home automation system, SHAS, enables different users to automate smart-home tasks. A smart home has a physical address and several rooms. A room may contain various kinds of sensors and actuator devices, also called controllers, such as temperature or movement sensors and light or lock controllers. Every sensor and actuator has a unique device identifier. Activating or deactivating a new sensor or actuator is detected by SHAS, which updates its infrastructure map accordingly.

During operation, sensor devices supply readings periodically. Each reading records a measured value and a timestamp. Actuators can receive commands from a predefined set, such as lockDoor and turnOnHeating. A command includes a timestamp and a status such as requested, completed, or failed. SHAS keeps all of a home's sensor readings and control commands in an activity log.

The owner can configure and manage relevant alerts for the home through automation rules. Each rule combines a precondition with an action. A precondition is a Boolean expression made of relational terms joined by AND, OR, or NOT. Its atomic terms can reference rooms, sensors, actuators, readings, and control commands. An action consists of a sequence of control commands. For instance, a rule might state that when Device #1244 reports an actualTemperature below 18 in the Living Room and the window is closed, turnOnHeating is to be executed in the Living Room.

Owners may create, modify, activate, and deactivate rules. A rule must be deactivated to be edited. Dependencies and conflicts among rules allow a complex hierarchy to be designed. Whenever an active rule is triggered, SHAS records the occurrence with a timestamp.
