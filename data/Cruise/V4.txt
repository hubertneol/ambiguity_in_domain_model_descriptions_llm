A particular provider organizes each cruise. Providers are described by a name and location. Cruises have a number and a name for identification, together with start and end dates. Many employees are needed for a cruise, and each employee has a name and social security number.

Several guests join a cruise. Each guest's name is known, and their VIP status is recorded. A guest may take part in multiple cruises.

For ships, the known details are name, deck count, length, and passenger count. The same ship may serve different cruises.

Bookings for cruises are made through travel agencies. A booking records its unique booking number and booking date, while an agency records its name and address.

Every cruise travels toward a particular destination area. Each such area has a name, and its sea's name is also recorded. Guests on a cruise can enjoy several shows, each described by a title and duration.

Each cruise has several guests, and guests may participate in several cruises. Several tickets are issued for a cruise, with every ticket belonging to precisely one cruise. There is no overall limit to a guest's tickets, but the guest may hold only one for any particular cruise.
