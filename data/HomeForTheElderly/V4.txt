The home for the elderly contains departments, each comprising several rooms, with several beds in each room. An inhabitant receives a bed for the duration of a stay. Beds belong to particular categories, and a price is associated with each category. The available stay categories are rest home (RH), rest & nursing home (RNH), and service flat (SF).

An inhabitant may have multiple concurrent stays. For example, John might live in a service flat, break his leg, and temporarily need rest & nursing accommodation. His service-flat stay would be suspended rather than terminated. Since he is already an inhabitant, this move to rest & nursing requires no proposal.

Anyone wishing to live in the home must register by answering questions about their basic identification details. The last registration step records those details in the system. Next, questions assess the person's abilities and need for assistance with everyday activities, including eating, dressing, and personal care such as showering. This assessment determines the appropriate stay type. The assessed person then joins the waiting list for that type. Registration date alone determines their position.

When a bed in the relevant category becomes available, it is offered to the highest-ranked person on that waiting list. The person has three days to notify the Rest & Nursing Home of acceptance or refusal. Refusing leaves the person on the list, and the next person seeking that stay type receives a new proposal. This continues until someone accepts. Acceptance prompts a fresh ability assessment to verify the proposed stay type. Once verified, an intake date is set and the actual stay begins on that date. If reassessment requires a different type, the proposal is recorded as invalidated and the requested type is changed, while the person's waiting-list position remains the same.

Stays are billed monthly. For simplicity, treat payments as events rather than objects. Receipt of a payment is recorded, and its amount is deducted from the invoice's outstanding balance. A zero balance causes the invoice to be marked paid. If a positive balance remains because too little was paid, the outstanding amount is updated and the invoice is resent showing what is still due. Overpayment instead causes the balance to be set to zero, the invoice to be marked paid, a credit note to be created, and an immediate refund to be made. Until an invoice is marked paid, its contents may be changed, for example to correct invoiced items following a complaint by an inhabitant or their representative.

Invoicing continues after a stay ends, whether through death or a move elsewhere, until every invoice is paid. The stay type determines the billed amount. If a service-flat stay continues during a temporary rest & nursing stay, for example, both are invoiced, with a discount applied to the service-flat stay.
